**Common University Entrance Test (CUET): A pathway to postgraduate mathematics**

Would you like to continue studying mathematics beyond your B.Sc., explore new ideas and learn in a university environment? Start preparing steadily, using your undergraduate coursework as the foundation. A carefully planned application strategy is just as valuable as examination preparation.

This page focuses on **CUET-PG Mathematics** for students seeking postgraduate study. CUET is the **Common University Entrance Test**; it is not limited to central universities. NTA conducts the examination, while participating institutions manage their admissions.

**Last reviewed: 23 September 2026.** The examination details below refer to the **2026 cycle**. For a later attempt, use that year's official bulletin and university prospectuses; do not assume that the dates, pattern or eligibility remain unchanged.

<h2 id="which-examination">CUET-UG or CUET-PG?</h2>

| Your next step | Where to start |
| --- | --- |
| Completing PUC/Class XII and seeking an undergraduate degree | [CUET-UG official website](https://cuet.nta.nic.in/). Check the required subject combination for each university and programme. |
| Completing a bachelor's degree and seeking a postgraduate mathematics programme | [CUET-PG official website](https://exams.nta.nic.in/cuet-pg/). Check the programme's required test paper and eligibility. |

For CUET-PG, the Mathematics paper is **SCQP19**. Select a paper based on the university's programme mapping, not just a similar-sounding course name.

<h2 id="examination-pattern">CUET-PG Mathematics: 2026 Pattern</h2>

| Item | 2026 details |
| --- | --- |
| Test paper | Mathematics — SCQP19 |
| Mode | Computer-based test |
| Questions | 75 subject-specific multiple-choice questions |
| Duration | 90 minutes |
| Maximum marks | 300 |
| Correct response | +4 marks |
| Incorrect response | −1 mark |
| Unattempted response | 0 marks |
| Language | English and Hindi |

Sources: [2026 bulletin](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s388a839f2f6f1427879fc33ee4acf4f66/uploads/2025/12/202512161583029269.pdf) and [SCQP19 syllabus](https://exams.nta.nic.in/cuet-pg/syllabus/science/).

For ordinary practice questions, calculate your score as **4 × correct answers − incorrect answers**. Official handling of dropped questions or revised keys follows NTA's rules.

**Practice suggestion:** A 90-minute paper with 75 questions averages 72 seconds per question. This is a planning average, not a time limit for each question. Begin with questions you can solve confidently and return to longer ones.

<h2 id="mathematics-syllabus">Mathematics Syllabus: Where to Begin</h2>

The following is a brief topic map, not a replacement for the [official syllabus](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s388a839f2f6f1427879fc33ee4acf4f66/uploads/2025/12/202512131193303366.pdf).

| Area | Revision focus |
| --- | --- |
| Abstract algebra | Groups, homomorphisms, quotient structures, rings, ideals and fields |
| Linear algebra | Vector spaces, linear maps, matrices, rank, eigenvalues and eigenvectors |
| Real analysis | Convergence, continuity, differentiation, power series and properties of subsets of the real line |
| Multivariable calculus | Partial derivatives, differentiability and constrained extrema |
| Complex analysis | Analytic functions, complex integration and fundamental integral theorems |
| Integral calculus | Definite and multiple integrals, changes of order and geometric applications |
| Differential equations | First-order equations and higher-order linear equations |
| Vector calculus | Vector operators, line/surface integrals and integral theorems |
| Linear programming | Convexity, feasible solutions, formulation, graphical solutions and simplex method |

**Make a personal checklist:** For every syllabus item, record whether you can explain the definition, give an example, solve a standard problem and handle a timed question. Revisit weak areas weekly.

<h2 id="university-choices">Eligibility and University Choices</h2>

Eligibility to sit the entrance examination and eligibility for a particular degree are separate checks. Before applying, read the programme's current requirements for qualifying degree, mathematics coursework, minimum marks and completion deadlines.

Use these official starting points to investigate postgraduate opportunities. This is a shortlist for exploration, not a claim that every mathematics-related programme at these institutions uses the same entrance paper.

| Institution or directory | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| [Central University of Karnataka](https://cuk.ac.in/) | PG admission notices, prospectus, eligibility, seat matrix and merit lists |
| [University of Hyderabad](https://acad.uohyd.ac.in/) | PG admission section, prospectus and programme requirements |

For example, Delhi's admission portal separately lists CUET requirements, PG registration and allocation information. **Do not assume that submitting the NTA form also registers you for university counselling.**

Create a comparison sheet with these columns:

**University | Programme | Required paper | Eligibility | Seats | Fees/hostel costs | Application deadline | Admission portal | Current status**

Choose programmes by their curriculum, faculty interests, affordability and learning environment as well as reputation. Investigate financial assistance through each university; do not assume that a CUET score automatically brings a scholarship.

**An answer key is not a complete question paper.** Keep your question paper or response sheet when NTA makes it available. Candidate-login links may be accessible only during the announced window.

### Supplementary question-paper archive

[AglaSem — CUET-PG Mathematics question papers](https://admission.aglasem.com/cuet-pg-question-paper-maths/) provides a third-party collection for practice. Check the date, shift and subject printed inside each document: some year labels and repeated links on the collection page are inconsistent. Use NTA's final key for verification wherever possible. Third-party solutions are not official NTA explanations.

### How to get more value from each paper

1. Confirm the paper is **PG Mathematics**, not CUET-UG Mathematics or another subject.
2. Attempt it without solutions, under a suitable time limit for that year's pattern.
3. Match question IDs and the correct date/shift when using the final key. Do not assume a reordered copy uses the same question numbering.
4. Record every error as a concept gap, calculation mistake, misreading or time-management issue.
5. Rework missed questions after a few days without looking at the solution.

Historical papers help identify recurring ideas, but their topic distribution is not a promise about the next examination. Avoid treating coaching-site weightage predictions as an official blueprint.

<h2 id="preparation-plan">A Practical Preparation Plan</h2>

The plan below is a suggested study framework, not an official timetable. Adjust it to your background, semester examinations and available time.

| Phase | Suggested activity |
| --- | --- |
| Weeks 1–2 | Read the syllabus, shortlist programmes, attempt a diagnostic paper and begin linear algebra revision. |
| Weeks 3–4 | Work on real analysis and calculus; maintain a notebook of definitions and counterexamples. |
| Weeks 5–6 | Revise abstract algebra and complex analysis; connect theorems with concrete examples. |
| Weeks 7–8 | Cover differential equations, vector calculus and linear programming; revisit omitted syllabus items. |
| Weeks 9–10 | Attempt mixed-topic sets and previous-year papers; repair recurring weaknesses. |
| Weeks 11–12 | Take timed mock papers, review errors and revise concise notes. Check application tasks and deadlines. |

A possible two-hour study session:

- **40 minutes:** study a concept and its examples.
- **60 minutes:** solve problems independently.
- **20 minutes:** correct mistakes and revisit earlier questions.

Begin with your B.Sc. notes and library textbooks. For analysis, collect examples showing why theorem hypotheses matter. For algebra, test claims on small groups and matrices. For calculus, check domains, signs and boundary cases before selecting an answer.

### Preparing for IIT JAM and CUET-PG together

Use the intersection of the two official syllabi for shared preparation. Maintain a separate checklist for topics unique to each examination and practise each paper in its own format. Do not assume that completing a JAM course covers all of SCQP19.

<h2 id="admissions">Applications, Counselling and Cut-offs</h2>

### Your application checklist

- Read the latest bulletin and each target university's prospectus.
- Confirm the paper code before paying the examination fee.
- Prepare the requested photograph, signature, identification and academic records in the specified formats.
- Check applicable category, disability and other certificate requirements directly against the instructions.
- Save confirmation pages, receipts and login details securely.
- Track examination registration and university admission deadlines separately.
- After results, complete each required university registration and preference submission.
- Monitor verification, allocation, acceptance and fee-payment deadlines until admission is complete.

### Understanding cut-offs

Look for **programme-specific, category-specific and round-specific** allocation scores or merit lists on university admission portals. For example, [Delhi's admission portal](https://admission.uod.ac.in/) publishes minimum allocation scores for different rounds.

For comparisons, record the year, institution, programme, category, round and score scale. Do not compare a percentile directly with marks, or an early-round allocation with a later vacancy round. A previous closing score is a reference point, not a guaranteed score for future admission.

<summary><strong>Can I prepare without paid coaching?</strong></summary>

<p>Build understanding first. Definitions, examples and counterexamples help you judge unfamiliar statements. Use timed questions to improve speed after you understand the underlying mathematics.</p>

## A Message to Students

Your preparation can begin with the mathematics you are learning today. Ask questions in class, solve problems regularly and keep track of what you have understood. Progress comes from returning to difficult ideas with patience and purpose. Aim widely, prepare carefully and give yourself the opportunity to study mathematics further.
